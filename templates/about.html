Why this exists

Nobody teaches this at the right time

School covers plenty of things you'll use one day. It mostly skips the ones you need next week: whether to buy the thing, how to make your money last the month, why the same phone costs more on one site than another.

By the time most people learn it, they already have habits that are annoying to undo. Starting at 15 is easier than fixing it at 25.

The advice that exists doesn't fit

It's either too basic to help ("save more") or it assumes you already know what an expense ratio is. Neither one gets you any closer to a decision.

MoBo goes the other way. It asks about your actual situation, uses amounts you'd recognise, and gives you something you can do today.

Three rules I keep coming back to

1

Plain words

If a word needs a glossary, the sentence needs rewriting. Lessons use things you actually run into: phone bills, snacks, subscriptions, a first job.

2

Built around you

Your roadmap comes from what you wrote about yourself. Two people who start in different places get different paths.

3

Honest over pushy

MoBo isn't selling anything and doesn't earn a cut. Sometimes the right answer is "wait and save", and it says so.
